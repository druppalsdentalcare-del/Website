Visiting the clinic

How do I book an appointment?

Use the Book Appointment button on any page, call us, or message us on WhatsApp. Choose your treatment and preferred time and our team will confirm your slot.

What should I bring to my first visit?

Bring a list of medicines you take, any previous dental X-rays or reports, and details of medical conditions such as diabetes, heart problems, pregnancy or allergies.

Do you treat dental emergencies?

Yes. We keep priority slots every day for severe toothache, swelling, broken or knocked-out teeth. Call or WhatsApp us and we'll see you as soon as possible.

Do you treat children?

Yes — we see patients of all ages, from a baby's first dental visit to seniors needing dentures or implants.

Comfort & safety

I'm scared of the dentist. Can you help?

Dental anxiety is very common and nothing to be embarrassed about. Tell us — we will explain each step, agree on a 'stop' signal, use effective numbing and take breaks whenever you need. Many anxious patients find that modern dentistry is far gentler than they expected.

How do you sterilise instruments?

Reusable instruments are cleaned, sealed in pouches and sterilised in a Class-B autoclave, with sterilisation monitoring. Single-use items such as needles, gloves, suction tips and patient bibs are discarded after each patient. Surfaces are disinfected between appointments.

Is dental treatment safe during pregnancy?

Routine check-ups, cleanings and necessary treatment are safe and encouraged, ideally in the second trimester — pregnancy hormones increase the risk of gum disease. Always tell us if you are pregnant; we avoid non-urgent X-rays and choose medicines carefully.

I have diabetes / heart disease / take blood thinners. Can I get treatment?

Yes, with appropriate precautions. Tell us about your conditions and medicines. For some procedures we may coordinate with your physician — never stop blood thinners on your own.

Costs & planning

How much will my treatment cost?

Cost depends on the treatment, the number of teeth, materials chosen (for example zirconia vs metal-ceramic crowns, or implant brand) and case complexity. After your examination we give you a written treatment plan with transparent costs before starting anything.

Do you offer EMI or payment plans?

Ask our front desk about the payment options currently available for larger treatments such as implants, aligners and smile makeovers.

Does health insurance cover dental treatment?

Most standard health insurance policies in India cover dental treatment only if it results from an accident or requires hospitalisation. Some policies and corporate plans include OPD dental cover — check your policy and we can provide the necessary bills and reports.

Everyday oral care

How should I brush my teeth correctly?

Brush twice a day for two minutes with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste. Angle the bristles at 45° to the gumline and use gentle circular strokes. Spit, don't rinse, so fluoride keeps working. Clean between teeth once a day with floss or interdental brushes.

Is an electric toothbrush better than a manual one?

Both work well with good technique. Electric toothbrushes (especially oscillating-rotating ones) can remove slightly more plaque and are helpful for people with braces or limited dexterity.

Why are my teeth sensitive?

Common causes include enamel wear, receding gums, cavities, cracked teeth or recent whitening. A sensitivity toothpaste helps mild cases, but have it checked to rule out decay or cracks.

What causes bad breath?

Most bad breath comes from bacteria on the tongue and between teeth, gum disease, dry mouth or decay. Cleaning your tongue, flossing and treating gum disease usually resolve it; occasionally a medical cause needs to be ruled out.

How often should I replace my toothbrush?

Every 3 months, or sooner if the bristles are frayed or after an illness.

Dental Check-up & Cleaning Treatment guide →

How often should I get my teeth cleaned?

For most people every 6 months. If you have gum disease, diabetes, braces or a history of heavy tartar, your dentist may recommend every 3–4 months.

Does teeth scaling damage enamel or loosen teeth?

No. Scaling removes only tartar, not enamel. Teeth can feel slightly 'loose' or sensitive afterwards because the tartar that was bridging them is gone and gums are healing — this settles within days as gums tighten.

Is dental cleaning painful?

Most patients feel only vibration and water spray. If your gums are inflamed or you have sensitivity, we can use a numbing gel or local anaesthetic to keep you comfortable.

Are dental X-rays safe?

Yes. Digital dental X-rays use a very low dose of radiation — roughly comparable to a few hours of natural background radiation. We take them only when clinically needed.

Why do my gums bleed when I brush?

Bleeding gums are usually an early sign of gingivitis caused by plaque build-up. A professional cleaning and better home care typically resolve it within 1–2 weeks.

Tooth-Coloured Fillings Treatment guide →

How long do tooth-coloured fillings last?

Composite fillings typically last 7–10 years or longer with good oral hygiene and regular check-ups. Large fillings on back teeth that take heavy biting forces may need replacement sooner.

Can I eat right after a filling?

Yes. Composite fillings are fully hardened with a curing light before you leave. Just wait for the anaesthetic to wear off so you don't bite your lip or cheek.

Should I replace my old silver (amalgam) fillings?

Only if they are cracked, leaking or have decay around them, or if you want them replaced for cosmetic reasons. Intact amalgam fillings do not need to be removed for safety reasons alone.

Do cavities go away on their own?

No. Very early enamel demineralisation (white spots) can sometimes be reversed with fluoride, but once a cavity forms it will only grow and must be treated.

Root Canal Treatment (RCT) Treatment guide →

Is root canal treatment painful?

Modern root canal treatment is done under effective local anaesthesia and most patients say it feels similar to getting a filling. It actually relieves the pain caused by the infected nerve. Some tenderness for 2–3 days afterwards is normal and managed with ordinary painkillers.

Can a root canal be done in one sitting?

Yes, in many cases. Single-sitting RCT is possible when the infection is controlled and the canals are straightforward. Teeth with large abscesses or complex anatomy may need 2–3 visits for the best long-term result.

Do I need a crown after root canal treatment?

Back teeth (premolars and molars) almost always need a crown because a root-canal-treated tooth becomes more brittle and can crack under biting forces. Some front teeth with minimal damage can be restored with a filling alone.

How long does a root canal treated tooth last?

With a well-fitting crown and good oral hygiene, a root-canal-treated tooth can last a lifetime. Success rates for properly done RCT are high — commonly reported at around 85–95%.

Is extraction better than a root canal?

Usually not. Extracting a tooth leads to shifting teeth and bone loss, and replacing it with an implant or bridge costs more. Saving your natural tooth with RCT is generally the preferred option when the tooth is restorable.

What is re-root canal treatment?

If a previously treated tooth becomes infected again — due to missed canals, leakage or new decay — the old filling material can be removed and the canals cleaned and resealed. This is called re-RCT or retreatment.

Tooth Extraction & Wisdom Teeth Treatment guide →

Should all wisdom teeth be removed?

No. Wisdom teeth that have fully erupted, are healthy and can be cleaned properly can stay. Removal is advised for wisdom teeth that are impacted, repeatedly infected, decayed, or damaging the tooth in front of them.

How painful is wisdom tooth removal?

The procedure itself is painless under local anaesthesia. Afterwards, expect swelling and soreness that peaks around day 2–3 and is well controlled with prescribed medicines. Most people feel much better within a week.

What is a dry socket?

Dry socket happens when the blood clot in the extraction site is dislodged, exposing bone and causing intense pain 2–4 days after extraction. Avoiding smoking, straws and vigorous rinsing reduces the risk; if it occurs, we treat it with a soothing medicated dressing.

What should I eat after a tooth extraction?

For the first 24 hours choose soft, cool foods such as curd, ice cream, smoothies (without a straw), dal, khichdi and mashed potatoes. Avoid hot, spicy, crunchy foods and alcohol for a few days.

Do I need to replace an extracted tooth?

Usually yes, except wisdom teeth. A gap causes neighbouring teeth to tilt and opposite teeth to over-erupt, affecting your bite. Implants, bridges and dentures are the common options.

Gum Disease Treatment Treatment guide →

Can gum disease be cured?

Gingivitis is fully reversible with professional cleaning and good home care. Periodontitis can't be fully reversed once bone is lost, but it can be stopped and controlled with treatment and regular maintenance.

Is deep cleaning different from regular cleaning?

Yes. Regular cleaning removes deposits above and just below the gumline. Deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) reaches deep into gum pockets to clean the root surfaces, usually under local anaesthesia, one section of the mouth at a time.

Can receding gums grow back?

Gums don't grow back on their own, but recession can be stopped, and gum grafting procedures can cover exposed roots to reduce sensitivity and improve appearance.

Are loose teeth from gum disease always lost?

Not necessarily. If enough bone remains, treating the infection and splinting the teeth can stabilise them for many years.

Kids' Dentistry Treatment guide →

When should my child first visit the dentist?

By their first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth appearing. Early visits are mostly preventive and help your child get comfortable with the dentist.

Why treat baby teeth if they will fall out anyway?

Baby molars stay until around age 10–12. Untreated decay can cause pain, abscesses that harm the developing adult tooth, and early loss that leads to crowding of permanent teeth.

What are dental sealants?

Sealants are thin protective coatings painted onto the grooves of back teeth, where most childhood cavities start. They are painless, quick and can significantly reduce cavities in those teeth.

When should my child get braces checked?

An orthodontic evaluation around age 7 is recommended. Some problems, such as jaw growth discrepancies or crossbites, are easier to correct while children are still growing.

Emergency Dental Care Treatment guide →

What should I do if a tooth is knocked out?

Pick it up by the crown (not the root), rinse gently with milk or saline if dirty, and try to push it back into the socket. If you can't, store it in milk or inside your cheek and see a dentist within 30–60 minutes. Do not scrub it or let it dry out.

How can I relieve a toothache at home?

Rinse with warm salt water, floss gently to remove trapped food and take an over-the-counter painkiller such as paracetamol or ibuprofen (if safe for you). Never place aspirin on the gum. See a dentist as soon as possible — painkillers don't treat the cause.

Is facial swelling from a tooth dangerous?

It can be. A dental abscess can spread. See a dentist the same day; if swelling affects your eye, breathing or swallowing, or you have a high fever, go to a hospital emergency department immediately.

My crown fell off — what should I do?

Keep the crown safe and call us. Avoid chewing on that side. A temporary dental cement from a pharmacy can hold it until your appointment, but don't use household glue.

Crowns & Bridges Treatment guide →

Which crown is best — zirconia, E-max or metal-ceramic?

Zirconia is extremely strong and ideal for back teeth and bridges. E-max offers the best translucency for front teeth. Metal-ceramic (PFM) is economical and durable but may show a grey line at the gum over time. Your dentist will recommend the best option for each tooth.

How long do dental crowns last?

With good oral hygiene and regular check-ups, crowns commonly last 10–15 years, and many last much longer.

Crown or implant — what is better for a missing tooth?

A bridge requires trimming the adjacent teeth to support it, whereas an implant replaces the tooth independently and preserves bone. Implants are often preferred when the neighbouring teeth are healthy; bridges are a good option when those teeth already need crowns or implants aren't suitable.

Does getting a crown hurt?

No. Tooth preparation is done under local anaesthesia. Some sensitivity for a few days after the temporary crown is normal.

Dental Implants Treatment guide →

Is dental implant surgery painful?

Implant placement is performed under local anaesthesia, so you shouldn't feel pain during the procedure. Most patients report less discomfort afterwards than they expected — similar to a tooth extraction — manageable with routine painkillers for a few days.

How long do dental implants last?

The implant itself can last 25 years or a lifetime with good oral hygiene and regular check-ups. The crown on top may need replacement after 10–15 years due to normal wear.

Am I too old for dental implants?

There is no upper age limit. What matters is your general health and bone quality. Many patients in their 70s and 80s successfully receive implants. Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or heavy smoking need to be managed first.

What if I don't have enough bone for an implant?

Bone grafting, sinus lift procedures, or shorter / angled implant techniques can often make implants possible even when bone has been lost.

Can I get teeth on the same day as the implant?

In selected cases with good bone quality, 'immediate loading' allows a temporary tooth or bridge to be fixed on the same day. Your dentist will tell you whether you are suitable after a 3D scan.

Implant vs bridge vs denture — which is better?

Implants are the most natural and long-lasting and protect bone, but cost more upfront and need surgery. Bridges are fixed and quicker but involve trimming adjacent teeth. Dentures are the most economical and non-surgical but are removable and less stable.

Dentures Treatment guide →

How long does it take to get used to new dentures?

Most people adapt within 2–4 weeks. Start with soft food cut into small pieces, chew on both sides, and read aloud to practise speaking. Sore spots are common at first and are easily adjusted at the clinic.

Can loose dentures be fixed?

Yes. A loose denture can be relined to fit your gums better. For the most secure fit, 2–4 implants can be placed so the denture snaps on firmly (implant-supported overdenture).

Should I sleep with my dentures in?

It is best to remove dentures at night. This lets your gums rest and reduces the risk of fungal infections and bad breath.

What are flexible dentures?

Flexible dentures are made from a thin, bendable nylon-based material without metal clasps. They are lightweight, comfortable and aesthetic, and are a popular choice for partial dentures.

Full Mouth Rehabilitation Treatment guide →

How long does full mouth rehabilitation take?

It depends on what is needed. Cases without implants may be completed in a few weeks; cases with implants or gum surgery can take 3–6 months because bone and gums need time to heal.

Is full mouth rehabilitation painful?

Each procedure is carried out under local anaesthesia, and sedation can be discussed for anxious patients. Treatment is spread over appointments to keep each visit comfortable.

Can I get full mouth rehabilitation if I have no teeth?

Yes. For people with no teeth, implant-supported fixed teeth (such as All-on-4 or All-on-6) or implant-retained dentures can rebuild the full mouth.

Teeth Whitening Treatment guide →

Is professional teeth whitening safe?

Yes, when done or supervised by a dentist. Professional whitening does not damage enamel. Temporary sensitivity is the most common side-effect and usually resolves within 24–48 hours.

How long does teeth whitening last?

Results usually last 1–3 years. Coffee, tea, smoking and paan shorten this; occasional touch-ups with take-home trays help maintain the shade.

Will whitening work on crowns, veneers or fillings?

No. Whitening only lightens natural tooth structure. Existing crowns, veneers or fillings may need to be replaced afterwards to match your new shade.

Are whitening toothpastes and charcoal effective?

Whitening toothpastes remove some surface stains but don't change the natural colour of teeth. Charcoal products are abrasive and may wear enamel over time, so we don't recommend them.

Who should avoid teeth whitening?

Pregnant or breastfeeding women, children under 16, and people with untreated cavities, gum disease or very sensitive teeth should postpone whitening until advised by their dentist.

Dental Veneers Treatment guide →

Do veneers damage your natural teeth?

Porcelain veneers require removal of a very thin layer of enamel, which is irreversible — you will always need a veneer or crown on those teeth. 'No-prep' or minimal-prep veneers may be possible in some cases. Composite veneers usually need little or no reduction.

Porcelain or composite veneers — which is better?

Porcelain looks more natural, resists stains and lasts 10–15 years but costs more and needs lab fabrication. Composite is quicker (single visit), less expensive and repairable, but stains and chips more easily and lasts about 5–7 years.

Do veneers look fake?

Well-designed veneers shouldn't. Natural results come from careful smile design: realistic shade, translucency, texture and tooth proportions that suit your face.

Can veneers fix crooked teeth?

Veneers can mask mild crowding, rotation or gaps. Significantly crooked teeth are better treated first with aligners or braces.

Smile Makeover & Smile Design Treatment guide →

What is Digital Smile Design (DSD)?

Digital Smile Design uses photos, videos and software to plan your new smile based on your facial features. It helps you and your dentist agree on the result before treatment, making outcomes more predictable.

How long does a smile makeover take?

Simple makeovers using whitening and composite bonding can be completed in about a week. Makeovers with porcelain veneers take 2–3 weeks; those including aligners can take several months.

Can I get a smile makeover before my wedding?

Yes — start planning 3–6 months ahead if possible. This allows time for any alignment or gum treatment, and for veneers or whitening to be finalised well before photos.

Braces (Metal & Ceramic) Treatment guide →

Is there an age limit for braces?

No. While treatment is often started between 10 and 14, healthy adults of any age can have braces. Adult treatment is very common today.

Do braces hurt?

Getting braces fitted doesn't hurt. Teeth may feel sore for 3–5 days after placement and after adjustments; soft food and simple painkillers help. Wax can soothe any rubbing on the cheeks.

Metal or ceramic braces — which should I choose?

Metal braces are the most economical and robust. Ceramic braces are tooth-coloured and much less noticeable, but are slightly more expensive and need a little more care.

Why do I need to wear a retainer?

Teeth naturally tend to move back towards their old positions. Retainers hold them in place while bone and gums stabilise, and long-term night-time wear is usually recommended.

Clear Aligners Treatment guide →

Are clear aligners as effective as braces?

For many mild to moderate cases, yes. Complex cases involving large tooth movements or significant jaw discrepancies may be treated more predictably with braces. Your dentist will advise after assessment.

Can I eat with aligners on?

No. Remove aligners to eat and drink anything other than plain water, then brush before putting them back. This prevents staining and trapping food against your teeth.

Do clear aligners hurt?

You may feel pressure for a day or two when switching to a new set — a sign that they're working. Most people find aligners more comfortable than braces.

What happens if I don't wear my aligners enough?

Teeth won't move as planned and aligners may stop fitting, which can extend treatment or require new aligners. Consistent wear of 20–22 hours a day is essential.
