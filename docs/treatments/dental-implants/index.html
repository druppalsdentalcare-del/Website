Dental Implants

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone that fuses with the bone and supports a crown, bridge or denture. It is the closest replacement to a natural tooth, prevents bone loss and, with good care, can last 25 years or a lifetime. Success rates are commonly reported at over 95%.

What is Dental Implants?

Missing teeth affect how you eat, speak and smile — and the jawbone beneath a gap gradually shrinks. Dental implants replace the root of the tooth, stimulating the bone and giving a rock-solid foundation for a new tooth.

Implants can replace a single tooth without touching the neighbouring teeth, support a fixed bridge for several missing teeth, or anchor a full set of teeth (All-on-4 / All-on-6) for people who have lost all their teeth.

Signs you may need it

  • One or more missing teeth
  • Loose or uncomfortable dentures
  • A tooth that needs extraction
  • Difficulty chewing
  • Not wanting to trim healthy teeth for a bridge

The procedure, step by step

  1. 1

    3D planning

    A CBCT scan measures bone volume and nerve positions for precise, safe placement.

  2. 2

    Implant placement

    The titanium implant is placed in the jawbone under local anaesthesia.

  3. 3

    Osseointegration

    Over 2–4 months the bone fuses with the implant. A temporary tooth can be worn.

  4. 4

    Abutment & scan

    A connector (abutment) is attached and impressions are taken for the final crown.

  5. 5

    Final crown

    A custom crown is fixed on the implant — matching your natural teeth.

Benefits

Looks, feels and works like a natural tooth
Preserves jawbone and facial structure
No need to cut neighbouring teeth
Long-lasting, often for life

Recovery & aftercare

  • Soft diet for a few days after surgery
  • No smoking — it greatly increases implant failure risk
  • Clean around implants carefully, using interdental brushes
  • Attend regular check-ups and cleanings

Dental Implants: frequently asked questions

Is dental implant surgery painful?

Implant placement is performed under local anaesthesia, so you shouldn't feel pain during the procedure. Most patients report less discomfort afterwards than they expected — similar to a tooth extraction — manageable with routine painkillers for a few days.

How long do dental implants last?

The implant itself can last 25 years or a lifetime with good oral hygiene and regular check-ups. The crown on top may need replacement after 10–15 years due to normal wear.

Am I too old for dental implants?

There is no upper age limit. What matters is your general health and bone quality. Many patients in their 70s and 80s successfully receive implants. Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or heavy smoking need to be managed first.

What if I don't have enough bone for an implant?

Bone grafting, sinus lift procedures, or shorter / angled implant techniques can often make implants possible even when bone has been lost.

Can I get teeth on the same day as the implant?

In selected cases with good bone quality, 'immediate loading' allows a temporary tooth or bridge to be fixed on the same day. Your dentist will tell you whether you are suitable after a 3D scan.

Implant vs bridge vs denture — which is better?

Implants are the most natural and long-lasting and protect bone, but cost more upfront and need surgery. Bridges are fixed and quicker but involve trimming adjacent teeth. Dentures are the most economical and non-surgical but are removable and less stable.

How much does it cost?

The cost of dental implants depends on your diagnosis, the number of teeth involved and the materials chosen. After your examination you'll receive a clear, written estimate before treatment starts. Book a consultation to get your personalised plan.
