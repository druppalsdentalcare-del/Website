Root Canal Treatment (RCT)

Root canal treatment (RCT) saves a badly decayed or infected tooth by removing the inflamed nerve (pulp), cleaning and sealing the canals and then protecting the tooth with a crown. With modern anaesthesia and rotary instruments it feels similar to getting a filling and is often completed in 1–2 visits.

What is Root Canal Treatment?

Inside every tooth is a soft core called the pulp, containing nerves and blood vessels. When deep decay, a crack or trauma lets bacteria reach the pulp, it becomes inflamed or infected — causing severe toothache, swelling or an abscess.

Root canal treatment removes the infected pulp, disinfects the canals and seals them, eliminating pain while preserving your natural tooth. Saving your own tooth is almost always better than extraction and replacement.

Signs you may need it

  • Severe or throbbing toothache, especially at night
  • Lingering sensitivity to hot or cold
  • Pain when biting or chewing
  • Swelling or a pimple on the gum
  • A darkened or discoloured tooth

The procedure, step by step

  1. 1

    Diagnosis & X-ray

    We confirm pulp involvement and map the root canals.

  2. 2

    Anaesthesia & isolation

    The tooth is numbed and isolated with a rubber dam to keep it clean.

  3. 3

    Cleaning & shaping

    Rotary instruments remove the infected pulp and shape the canals; an apex locator measures their length precisely.

  4. 4

    Disinfection & sealing

    Canals are irrigated with antibacterial solutions and filled with gutta-percha.

  5. 5

    Crown

    A crown is placed to protect the treated tooth from cracking and restore full chewing.

Benefits

Relieves toothache quickly
Saves your natural tooth
Prevents infection spreading to the jaw
Restores normal biting and appearance

Recovery & aftercare

  • Avoid chewing hard food on the tooth until the crown is placed
  • Take prescribed pain relief or antibiotics as directed
  • Get the final crown done on time — an uncrowned RCT tooth can fracture

Root Canal Treatment: frequently asked questions

Is root canal treatment painful?

Modern root canal treatment is done under effective local anaesthesia and most patients say it feels similar to getting a filling. It actually relieves the pain caused by the infected nerve. Some tenderness for 2–3 days afterwards is normal and managed with ordinary painkillers.

Can a root canal be done in one sitting?

Yes, in many cases. Single-sitting RCT is possible when the infection is controlled and the canals are straightforward. Teeth with large abscesses or complex anatomy may need 2–3 visits for the best long-term result.

Do I need a crown after root canal treatment?

Back teeth (premolars and molars) almost always need a crown because a root-canal-treated tooth becomes more brittle and can crack under biting forces. Some front teeth with minimal damage can be restored with a filling alone.

How long does a root canal treated tooth last?

With a well-fitting crown and good oral hygiene, a root-canal-treated tooth can last a lifetime. Success rates for properly done RCT are high — commonly reported at around 85–95%.

Is extraction better than a root canal?

Usually not. Extracting a tooth leads to shifting teeth and bone loss, and replacing it with an implant or bridge costs more. Saving your natural tooth with RCT is generally the preferred option when the tooth is restorable.

What is re-root canal treatment?

If a previously treated tooth becomes infected again — due to missed canals, leakage or new decay — the old filling material can be removed and the canals cleaned and resealed. This is called re-RCT or retreatment.

How much does it cost?

The cost of root canal treatment (rct) depends on your diagnosis, the number of teeth involved and the materials chosen. After your examination you'll receive a clear, written estimate before treatment starts. Book a consultation to get your personalised plan.
