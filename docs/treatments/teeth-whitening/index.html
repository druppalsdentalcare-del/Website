Teeth Whitening

Professional teeth whitening uses dentist-applied peroxide gels to lighten natural tooth colour by several shades. In-office whitening takes about 60–90 minutes; take-home trays take 1–2 weeks. It is safe when supervised, and results typically last 1–3 years depending on diet and habits. It does not change the colour of crowns or fillings.

What is Teeth Whitening?

Teeth naturally darken with age, and stains from tea, coffee, red wine, paan and tobacco build up over time. Professional whitening safely breaks down these stains for a brighter, younger-looking smile.

Unlike over-the-counter products, professional whitening is customised: we protect your gums, choose the right gel strength and monitor sensitivity for predictable, even results.

Signs you may need it

  • Yellowish or dull teeth
  • Stains from tea, coffee or tobacco
  • Preparing for a wedding or special event
  • Wanting a brighter smile without veneers

Recovery & aftercare

  • Follow a 'white diet' for 48 hours — avoid tea, coffee, turmeric, colas and red sauces
  • Use sensitivity toothpaste if needed
  • Avoid smoking to maintain results

Teeth Whitening: frequently asked questions

Is professional teeth whitening safe?

Yes, when done or supervised by a dentist. Professional whitening does not damage enamel. Temporary sensitivity is the most common side-effect and usually resolves within 24–48 hours.

How long does teeth whitening last?

Results usually last 1–3 years. Coffee, tea, smoking and paan shorten this; occasional touch-ups with take-home trays help maintain the shade.

Will whitening work on crowns, veneers or fillings?

No. Whitening only lightens natural tooth structure. Existing crowns, veneers or fillings may need to be replaced afterwards to match your new shade.

Are whitening toothpastes and charcoal effective?

Whitening toothpastes remove some surface stains but don't change the natural colour of teeth. Charcoal products are abrasive and may wear enamel over time, so we don't recommend them.

Who should avoid teeth whitening?

Pregnant or breastfeeding women, children under 16, and people with untreated cavities, gum disease or very sensitive teeth should postpone whitening until advised by their dentist.

How much does it cost?

The cost of teeth whitening depends on your diagnosis, the number of teeth involved and the materials chosen. After your examination you'll receive a clear, written estimate before treatment starts. Book a consultation to get your personalised plan.
