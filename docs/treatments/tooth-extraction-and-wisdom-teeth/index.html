Tooth Extraction & Wisdom Teeth in New Delhi

Tooth extraction removes a tooth that cannot be saved, or a wisdom tooth that is impacted, painful or causing damage. It is done under local anaesthesia; simple extractions take 15–30 minutes and most people return to normal activities within 1–3 days.

What is Tooth Extraction & Wisdom Teeth?

We always try to save a tooth first. But when a tooth is broken beyond repair, severely infected, loose due to gum disease or needs to be removed for orthodontic treatment, a careful extraction is the healthiest choice.

Wisdom teeth (third molars) often lack space to erupt properly. Impacted wisdom teeth can cause recurrent infection, pain, damage to the neighbouring tooth and cysts. We assess them with X-rays and remove them using minimally invasive surgical techniques when needed.

Signs you may need it

  • Pain or swelling at the back of the jaw
  • Repeated gum infection over a partly erupted wisdom tooth
  • A tooth broken below the gumline
  • A very loose tooth
  • Food trapping behind the last molar

The procedure, step by step

  1. 1

    Assessment

    X-ray (or CBCT scan for complex wisdom teeth) to check roots and nearby nerves.

  2. 2

    Anaesthesia

    The area is completely numbed. You feel pressure, not pain.

  3. 3

    Gentle removal

    The tooth is loosened and removed; impacted teeth may be sectioned for a smaller, kinder procedure.

  4. 4

    Socket care

    The area is cleaned and stitched if needed; a gauze pack helps clot formation.

  5. 5

    Aftercare & replacement plan

    We give written instructions and discuss replacement options if needed.

Benefits

Ends pain and recurring infection
Protects neighbouring teeth
Prevents cysts and bone damage from impacted teeth
Creates space for orthodontic treatment

Recovery & aftercare

  • Bite on gauze for 30–45 minutes
  • No spitting, rinsing, straws or smoking for 24 hours
  • Eat soft, cool food for the first day
  • Use ice packs for swelling; warm salt-water rinses from day 2

Tooth Extraction & Wisdom Teeth: frequently asked questions

Should all wisdom teeth be removed?

No. Wisdom teeth that have fully erupted, are healthy and can be cleaned properly can stay. Removal is advised for wisdom teeth that are impacted, repeatedly infected, decayed, or damaging the tooth in front of them.

How painful is wisdom tooth removal?

The procedure itself is painless under local anaesthesia. Afterwards, expect swelling and soreness that peaks around day 2–3 and is well controlled with prescribed medicines. Most people feel much better within a week.

What is a dry socket?

Dry socket happens when the blood clot in the extraction site is dislodged, exposing bone and causing intense pain 2–4 days after extraction. Avoiding smoking, straws and vigorous rinsing reduces the risk; if it occurs, we treat it with a soothing medicated dressing.

What should I eat after a tooth extraction?

For the first 24 hours choose soft, cool foods such as curd, ice cream, smoothies (without a straw), dal, khichdi and mashed potatoes. Avoid hot, spicy, crunchy foods and alcohol for a few days.

Do I need to replace an extracted tooth?

Usually yes, except wisdom teeth. A gap causes neighbouring teeth to tilt and opposite teeth to over-erupt, affecting your bite. Implants, bridges and dentures are the common options.

How much does it cost?

The cost of tooth extraction & wisdom teeth depends on your diagnosis, the number of teeth involved and the materials chosen. After your examination you’ll receive a clear, itemised estimate before treatment starts. Book a consultation to get your personalised plan.
