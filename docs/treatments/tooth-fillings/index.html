Tooth-Coloured Fillings in New Delhi

A tooth-coloured (composite) filling repairs a cavity in a single 30–60 minute visit. The decay is removed under local anaesthesia and the tooth is rebuilt with a resin that is colour-matched and bonded to the tooth, so it is nearly invisible.

What is Tooth-Coloured Fillings?

Cavities are caused by bacteria that dissolve tooth enamel. Left untreated, a small cavity grows into the nerve and turns a simple filling into a root canal treatment or extraction.

We use modern composite resin and glass-ionomer materials that bond to the tooth, preserve more natural structure than old silver (amalgam) fillings and match the shade of your teeth.

Signs you may need it

  • Sensitivity to sweets, hot or cold
  • A visible dark spot or hole
  • Food getting stuck between teeth
  • A chipped or broken tooth
  • An old filling that is cracked or leaking

The procedure, step by step

  1. 1

    Diagnosis

    Examination and X-ray confirm the size and depth of the cavity.

  2. 2

    Numbing

    A local anaesthetic is given so you feel no pain.

  3. 3

    Decay removal

    Only the decayed portion of the tooth is gently removed.

  4. 4

    Bonding & layering

    Shade-matched composite is placed in layers and hardened with a blue curing light.

  5. 5

    Shaping & polishing

    The filling is shaped to your bite and polished to a natural shine.

Recovery & aftercare

  • Wait until numbness wears off before eating, to avoid biting your cheek
  • Mild sensitivity for a few days is normal
  • Tell us if your bite feels high — a quick adjustment fixes it

Tooth-Coloured Fillings: frequently asked questions

How long do tooth-coloured fillings last?

Composite fillings typically last 7–10 years or longer with good oral hygiene and regular check-ups. Large fillings on back teeth that take heavy biting forces may need replacement sooner.

Can I eat right after a filling?

Yes. Composite fillings are fully hardened with a curing light before you leave. Just wait for the anaesthetic to wear off so you don't bite your lip or cheek.

Should I replace my old silver (amalgam) fillings?

Only if they are cracked, leaking or have decay around them, or if you want them replaced for cosmetic reasons. Intact amalgam fillings do not need to be removed for safety reasons alone.

Do cavities go away on their own?

No. Very early enamel demineralisation (white spots) can sometimes be reversed with fluoride, but once a cavity forms it will only grow and must be treated.

How much does it cost?

The cost of tooth-coloured fillings depends on your diagnosis, the number of teeth involved and the materials chosen. After your examination you’ll receive a clear, itemised estimate before treatment starts. Book a consultation to get your personalised plan.
